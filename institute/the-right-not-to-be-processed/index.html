Charter 0.4 / Right 20
Consent to preserve is not consent to process.

A system may be permitted to keep a record it is not permitted to use.

Anni McHenry Fieldlight Institute August 2026

The missing instruction

Keep this intact.
Do nothing with it.

Most consent systems can represent some combination of yes, no, expired, withdrawn, and deleted. They rarely represent preservation without interpretive surrender.

That omission forces a false choice: authorize use, or remove the record from custody altogether.

Negative Permission Is Not Null

A blank field says we do not know.
A denial says no.

When both are stored as the same null value, the system cannot faithfully represent participant intent. Refusal needs scope, duration, inheritance, auditability, and enforcement.

Operation-Level Authority

Possession authorizes none of these by itself.

The meaningful unit of consent is not the file. It is the operation performed on the file, by a named party, for a named purpose, within a named period.

inspectanalyzeinferindex embedlinkcombinesummarize quoteresurfacetrainevaluate publishgeneratederiveenrich

Preservation-only custody may permit named integrity operations—receive, hash, encrypt, copy, verify, migrate, return, delete—without creating a general right to inspect or use content.

Execution, Not Interface

The refusal must survive the button.

An interface can display a choice. Governance begins when the system still honors it at execution time.

  1. 01Record custody authority separately from processing authority.
  2. 02Express explicit denials instead of treating them as missing values.
  3. 03Attach permission to source, operation, purpose, actor, and period.
  4. 04Deny unlisted operations by default.
  5. 05Enforce the restriction at every human and machine access boundary.
  6. 06Propagate restrictions to copies, indexes, embeddings, links, and derivatives as specified.
  7. 07Log allowed, attempted, and blocked operations without exposing protected content.
  8. 08Preserve the permission state across tool, model, vendor, and custodian changes.

Current Implementation Pattern

Preserved. Registered. Related. Not opened for analysis.

Fieldlight's restricted relational archive already demonstrates the underlying pattern. A source can exist in custody, be registered at the stewardship layer, and be connected to a relationship record while its substantive contents remain outside cross-source analysis and public representation.

The archive can know that a source exists, where it came from, what relationship it belongs to, and what restrictions govern it—without treating possession as permission to compute over its contents.

Constitutional Consequence

Deletion is not the only form of agency.

Sometimes the record must remain while computation stops.
